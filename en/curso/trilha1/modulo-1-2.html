🐕 What Hermes is (and when to use it)

Where Hermes fits compared with Claude Code, OpenClaw, and IDEs. The idea that ties it all together: Hermes is your dog, not your hired help—it lives with you, knows you, and gets better year after year.

1

🐕 Hermes = your Labrador

The central analogy: Hermes is like your dog. It lives with you, gets to know you and improves year after year. It's not a tool you turn on and off: it's a long-term relationship.

🏡 What “living with you” means

  • •Persistence: remembers who you are between conversations
  • •Familiarity: learns your style, routines, and projects
  • •Evolution: it gets more useful each month, not less

💡 Practical tip

Treat Hermes as a long-term companion: invest in it early (memory, soul, integrations), and the returns grow over time.

2

👷 Claude Code = the contractor

O Claude Code is the contractor: excellent for a specific job, a precision tool. But it doesn’t live with you doesn’t even remind you next time.

✓ The contractor is great for

  • ✓Precision technical work (code)
  • ✓One-off tasks with a beginning, middle, and end
  • ✓Depth in a specific project

✗ What the contractor does NOT do

  • ✗Remember you in the next session
  • ✗Keep you company day to day
  • ✗Build a long-term relationship

Important: it’s not “better or worse” — they have different roles. Module 3.7 shows how to connect the two so they share memory.

3

🛋️ OpenClaw & anti-gravity

To complete the map: the OpenClaw is the roommate and the anti-gravity is your IDE/coding partner. Each piece of the ecosystem serves a purpose.

🐕

Hermes — your dog

Lives with you, knows you, and gets better over time. A persistent companion.

👷

Claude Code — the contractor

Precision tool for one job; it won't remind you later.

🛋️

OpenClaw — roommate

Shares the workspace with you in your workflow.

🧑‍💻

anti-gravity — your IDE/partner

The environment where you program side by side with AI.

4

📈 The More You Use It, the Better It Knows You

The essence of Hermes: the more you use it, the more it gets to know you — and the more useful it gets. The value compounds: it grows over time rather than appearing all at once on day one.

📊 Accumulation effect

  • •Day 1 — a generic agent, capable but unfamiliar with you
  • •Weeks later — knows your projects, contacts, and preferences
  • •Months later — anticipates what you need before you ask

💡 Practical tip

Don’t judge Hermes by your first use. The real benefit appears once it has built up context about you — memory (Track 2) is what powers that.

5

🏃 When to use Hermes (and when not to)

Simple rule: use Hermes when you are mobile — coffee shop, gym, on your phone. Use desktop tools when you’re on the desktop, with focus and keyboard.

✓ Prefer Hermes

  • ✓Away from your desk (coffee shop, gym, outdoors)
  • ✓On your phone while you're on the go
  • ✓Tasks that depend on your personal context

✗ Prefer desktop tools

  • ✗Long coding session at the desk
  • ✗When you need an IDE and keyboard
  • ✗Precision work on a technical project
6

🎨 Design ethos: living with you

Hermes’s design principle is live with you and improve over time — not to be a disposable tool. This ethos explains practically every other product decision.

🧬 The ethos in concrete decisions

  • •Local memory (Track 2) — to build up context about you
  • •Soul.md — to give it your personality and your way of doing things
  • •GitHub backup (Track 3) — so you never lose the relationship you've built

💡 Practical tip

Whenever a decision Hermes makes seems strange, ask: "does this help him live with me and improve?" — the answer usually explains why.

7

🧯 Common mistakes & the pocket guide

Before you wrap up: the most common pitfalls for beginners — and a quick reference for which tool to use in each situation.

✓ Do it

  • ✓Give it time: value grows with use
  • ✓Invest early in memory and soul
  • ✓Choose the tool based on the context (mobile vs. desktop)

✗ Avoid

  • ✗Expecting "magic" on day one
  • ✗Expecting Claude Code to remind you later
  • ✗Use Hermes for tasks that call for an IDE and keyboard

Pocket guide (which tool to use)

móvel / acompanha você / contexto pessoal  -> 🐕 Hermes
job técnico de precisão / sessão única      -> 👷 Claude Code
colega no fluxo de trabalho                 -> 🛋️ OpenClaw
