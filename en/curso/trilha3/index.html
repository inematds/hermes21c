3.1~25 min

🛡️ Security · Principle of least access

The house rules: give the agent only what it needs, and never expose secrets in chat.

What is:

Unlike a chatbot, an agent can send email, modify files, and act on your computer. Real access requires real care.

Why learn:

Before enabling any new capability, you need to understand what could go wrong.

Key concepts:

Don’t hand over the keys to the kingdom; enable capabilities gradually.

What is:

Give the agent only the permission the task requires — nothing more. This applies to Hermes and any system.

Why learn:

If something goes wrong, the damage is contained to a minimum.

Key concepts:

Reading ≠ writing ≠ sending; start restrictive and expand as needed.

What is:

Enable "read email" now, but hold off on "send/reply to email" until you trust how it behaves.

Why learn:

A mistaken send is irreversible; reading is safe.

Key concepts:

Leave irreversible actions for last; test in read-only mode first.

What is:

The chat is saved and indexed in memory; a secret pasted there gets stored, especially if there’s a daily backup.

Why learn:

Leaking the key gives access to your model and your money.

Key concepts:

Use environment variables; if one leaks, rotate it immediately.

What is:

When the agent asks for access, you decide: allow it once, for the session, or block it entirely.

Why learn:

Each level trades convenience for control.

Key concepts:

"Once" for the unknown; "never" for the dangerous.

What is:

The same principles protect you in any AI agent or system, not just Hermes.

Why learn:

The security habit is transferable and stays with you.

Key concepts:

Minimum access + secrets outside the chat = secure foundation.

⭐ Northstar (Goals) · Doesn't stop until it hits the goal

Instead of question→answer, you set a direction and the agent works in turns until it reaches the goal.

What is:

You set a goal, and the entire conversation exists to achieve it, not to answer a one-off question.

Why learn:

It's how to turn a chat into a purposeful mini-project.

Key concepts:

Goal > one-off answer; the agent pursues, it doesn't just react.

What is:

When you receive /goal, Hermes signs a budget of about 20 turns to reach a decision.

Why learn:

Knowing the limit helps you calibrate the size of the goal.

Key concepts:

Asks questions, doesn't get stuck in a loop; converges within the budget.

What is:

For goals that are too big, the super goals skill generates 4 to 10 actions (yours and the agent's) with a progress bar.

Why learn:

Not every goal fits into 20 turns; super goals break it into steps.

Key concepts:

Mission control on the dashboard; visible progress.

What is:

Commands to start, pause, and clear a goal at any time.

Why learn:

You stay in the driver’s seat even during an autonomous task.

Key concepts:

Pausing saves state; clearing resets the goal.

What is:

Chief wickham loops are goals that last weeks, for planning your life, not just a short project.

Why learn:

Connects the short term (turns) to the long term (life).

Key concepts:

Recurring review loop; a horizon of weeks.

What is:

Goals shine when there’s a decision to make or a short project to deliver—not for one-off questions.

Why learn:

Using goals for everything wastes turns.

Key concepts:

Deciding A vs. B is a perfect use case; “what time is it” isn’t.

👥 Sub-agents · Parallel team

Hermes spins up several subagents with fresh context and delegates — 6 working together can do in 1h what would take 6h.

What is:

Instead of doing everything in a single thread, Hermes becomes an orchestrator that delegates work to helpers.

Why learn:

Large tasks become manageable when broken into several small ones.

Key concepts:

Orchestrator + executors; divide and conquer.

What is:

Each sub-agent is created with its own clean context window, focused only on its subtask.

Why learn:

Lean context = better and cheaper answers.

Key concepts:

Isolation prevents confusion between tasks.

What is:

4 to 6 agents research at the same time and deliver together at the end, instead of one after another.

Why learn:

Parallelism is the power-user move that compresses time.

Key concepts:

Breadth > depth when tasks are independent.

What is:

"Research the best AI companies" spins up 2 sub-agents: one covers the US, the other the international market.

Why learn:

Shows how to divide a broad scope into clear slices.

Key concepts:

Split by dimension (region, topic); merge at the end.

What is:

The Hermes co-founder runs 12 parallel instances every day to build Hermes itself (issues, dogfooding, kanban).

Why learn:

It’s proof that parallelism scales far beyond 2 or 3.

Key concepts:

Dogfooding at scale; a fleet of agents.

What is:

Each agent can have a role: research, writing, design, scheduler — like roles on a real team.

Why learn:

Clear roles prevent two agents from doing the same thing.

Key concepts:

Specialization; each role with its ideal model.

💓 Heartbeat / Cron · Works while you sleep

The heartbeat that keeps the agent alive 24/7 and the schedules that trigger tasks automatically.

What is:

A cron job that keeps the agent awake all the time, instead of just reacting when you talk to it.

Why learn:

It's what separates a chatbot from an agent that operates on its own.

Key concepts:

Periodic heartbeat; continuous presence.

What is:

A sub-agent pings every few seconds to detect “zombie” (stalled) processes and recover them.

Why learn:

Without this, a stuck task would stay stuck forever.

Key concepts:

Stall detection; fresh agent reclaim; retries.

What is:

You schedule an action to run at an interval: "remind me in 30s that the sky is blue".

Why learn:

Automates reminders and routines without you opening the app.

Key concepts:

Time-based scheduling; runs on its own.

What is:

Every day at 8 a.m., Hermes scans email + calendar + what it knows about you and delivers 5 items that matter.

Why learn:

Shows how cron + memory become a proactive assistant.

Key concepts:

Source aggregation; daily curation.

What is:

With a heartbeat, the agent proactively checks in: "periodically ask me things."

Why learn:

It reverses the relationship: it reaches out to you, instead of just responding.

Key concepts:

Proactivity; agent initiative.

What is:

Each trigger uses tokens; too many scheduled tasks create costs and excessive notifications.

Why learn:

Connects to the budget module (3.5).

Key concepts:

Custom frequency; review active crons.

💰 Budget & Tokens · 73% Is Fixed Overhead

The dirty secret of agents: most of every request is fixed cost. Knowing this changes how you operate.

What is:

About 73% of each request is fixed overhead (system prompt, tools, memory) — only about 27% is your question.

Why learn:

Explains why short questions still cost a lot.

Key concepts:

High base cost; every call pays the “toll.”

What is:

The pocket guide: ~10 tokens equal about 7 words (≈70-75%).

Why learn:

Gives you a sense of the size before you send a huge block of text.

Key concepts:

A token ≈ a piece of a word; it’s easy to estimate.

What is:

Clear the session often, use the right model, don’t accumulate useless skills, and keep system prompts short.

Why learn:

Small habits cut the bill in half.

Key concepts:

One conversation, one goal; always start fresh.

What is:

Someone burned through 4 million tokens in 2 hours of light use; someone else spent 21,000 just asking about the weather because of a bug.

Why learn:

With an API key, money disappears fast if you don't keep an eye on it.

Key concepts:

Uncontrolled loops are expensive; monitor usage.

What is:

Be specific about the model: heavy reasoning on the expensive one, volume on the cheap or free one.

Why learn:

The wrong choice multiplies the cost without adding any benefit.

Key concepts:

Expensive only where it pays off; cheap everywhere else.

What is:

Set a cap (e.g., US$10/month) and the system stops when it reaches the limit.

Why learn:

A cap is your safety net against billing surprises.

Key concepts:

Hard limit; alert before it’s exceeded.

🌐 Operating System · The system for everything

Hermes isn't just a chatbot: it's a single dashboard for managing personas, memory, spending, goals, and connections.

What is:

Hermes can be the layer where you manage ALL your AI in one place — the "everything of AI".

Why learn:

Shifts the mindset from “chat app” to “operating system.”

Key concepts:

A hub, not a standalone feature.

What is:

Pantheon displays your personas (each with a job and model) visually within the OS.

Why learn:

Viewing the personas in one place makes it easier to choose who uses what.

Key concepts:

Personas as cards; model per persona.

What is:

A memory vault that retrieves any email, meeting, or note; you can connect NotebookLM.

Why learn:

Centralizes the knowledge the agent can consult.

Key concepts:

Universal recall; pluggable sources.

What is:

At a glance: available connections, model in use, memory, AI spending, gains from skills, and real-time usage.

Why learn:

Operating without a dashboard is flying blind.

Key concepts:

Unified view; live metrics.

What is:

The OS "dreams" up improvements at night and comes back with proactive suggestions the next day.

Why learn:

The system evolves on its own, without you asking.

Key concepts:

Self-optimization; ideas while you sleep.

What is:

Connect the OS to GitHub for a daily backup of the entire Hermes setup; if you lose your PC, restore everything.

Why learn:

Your OS is too valuable to die with the hardware.

Key concepts:

Daily snapshot; full restoration.

🔗 One Brain · Hermes + Claude Code

Connecting Hermes’s brain to Claude Code: shared memory and understanding complete the 21 concepts.

What is:

The central idea is to connect the Hermes brand/brain to Claude Code so they can share context.

Why learn:

Two isolated agents repeat work and contradict each other.

Key concepts:

One mind, two tools; context bridge.

What is:

Hermes knows your projects, clients, and decisions and is everywhere; Claude Code is the precision tool for building.

Why learn:

Each one shines in a role; knowing this helps you avoid using the wrong tool.

Key concepts:

Broad context vs. precision; ubiquitous vs. focused.

What is:

When connected, both can access what the other has done; what you say on one side reaches the other.

Why learn:

Shared memory is what makes the connection meaningful.

Key concepts:

Shared state; no silos.

What is:

If you tell Hermes one thing and Claude Code another, they diverge without a connection.

Why learn:

Divergence leads to rework and inconsistent decisions.

Key concepts:

Silo = contradiction; bridge = coherence.

What is:

This is concept #21: it ties together everything that came before into a single view.

Why learn:

Seeing the big picture prepares you to operate for real.

Key concepts:

From agent to OS; everything connected.

What is:

With the 21 concepts in hand, the next step is to build your own AI operating system.

Why learn:

Theory without practice doesn’t become an operation.

Key concepts:

Start small; connect; iterate.
