MODULE 3.2

⭐ Northstar (Goals) · Doesn't stop until it hits the goal

Most AI usage follows a question → answer pattern. The goal function turns this around: you set a direction, and the entire conversation exists to achieve it. The agent asks questions, makes decisions, and only stops when it reaches the goal.

1

🎯 A goal function: a guiding direction, not a question

Imagine you don’t ask, "give me information about A and B." Instead, you say: "choose between opportunity A and B"That’s a goal. The agent now has a clear, measurable objective, and each conversation turn helps move toward it.

Illustrative diagram · each turn pushes the conversation toward the guiding star

🧭 Question vs. Goal

A question ends with the answer. A goal only ends when the objective is achieved — even if that takes several turns and several questions from the agent to you.

2

🔢 The ~20-turn budget

When you receive /goal, Hermes "signs" a budget of about 20 turns to reach the decision. This keeps it from getting stuck in an infinite loop and gives you predictability: the goal fits in a short project, not an endless marathon.

Example command (illustrative)

/goal decide between job offer A (remote, US$X) and job offer B (on-site, US$Y)
# Hermes: "To decide, I need to know 3 things..."
# <asks questions, weighs criteria, recommends>
# Goal reached in 11 turns ✓

📊 Why a budget

  • No infinite loop — the agent converges within the limit.
  • Predictable cost — you know the approximate token count.
  • Focus — forces the agent to ask only what matters.
3

🚀 Super goals: 4 to 10 actions

Not every goal fits into 20 turns. “Plan a product launch” is too big. That’s what the skill is for super goals: you explain the goal, Hermes asks several questions, and generates 4 to 10 actions — some yours, some its — with a progress bar and mission control on the dashboard.

Mission control · illustrative recreation, not a real screenshot
1. Research competitors100%
2. Set the price (you decide)100%
3. Draft landing page copy60%
4. Schedule launch posts0%

💡 Practical tip

Super goals combine actions yours e of the agent. The ones that require human judgment (pricing, hiring) stay with you; the repetitive, research-heavy ones stay with it.

4

⌨️ /goal, /goal pause, /goal clear

You never lose the wheel. Three commands control every goal — start, pause, and clear.

1

/goal — starts

Sets the north star and opens up the turn budget. The conversation starts pursuing the goal.

2

/goal pause — pause

Freeze the goal by saving its state. You can work on something else and pick up where you left off.

3

/goal clear — clean

Reset the objective. Use this when the goal has changed or no longer makes sense — return to normal conversation mode.

5

🗓️ Wickham loops: medium-term goals

Goals solve short-term needs (a project, a decision). For the medium term, there are chief wickham loops: goals that last for weeks and help you plan your life, not just a task. It’s a recurring review loop—the agent tracks your progress over time.

✓ Good for Wickham loops

  • ✓"I want to change careers in the next 3 months."
  • ✓"Launch a product by the end of the quarter."
  • ✓"Improve my health with weekly check-ins."

✗ Better as a simple /goal

  • ✗"Choose between two vendors today."
  • ✗"Summarize this report now."
  • ✗Anything that ends in a session.
6

🧭 When to use goals (and when not to)

Goals cost turns and overhead. Using them for everything is wasteful. They shine when there’s a decision to make or a short project to deliver — not for one-off questions.

💡 Practical tip

Ask yourself: "does this have a clear criterion for ready?" If so, it’s a goal. If it’s just a quick question ("what time is it?"), it’s a normal conversation—don’t spend a 20-turn budget on it.

🧱 Anatomy of a good goal

A well-written goal uses fewer turns and converges faster. The difference comes down to three things: a clear objective, one definition of done and the constraints that matter. Too vague, and the agent burns turns asking basic questions.

✓ Sharpened goal

  • ✓"Decide between A and B by the end of the conversation, prioritizing cost."
  • ✓Has a clear completion criterion: a justified recommendation.
  • ✓Explicit constraint (cost) guides the questions.

✗ Vague goal

  • ✗"Help me with opportunity A and B." (Help how?)
  • ✗Without a completion criterion, the agent doesn't know when to stop.
  • ✗No constraints—it wastes turns asking the obvious.

💡 Practical tip

Mental model: "achieve X, knowing it’s done when Y, while respecting Z"Fill in X, Y, and Z before sending the /goal.
